When several or all teeth are missing, most people choose between removable dentures and fixed dental implants. Both replace teeth, but they feel and behave very differently. Here is an honest comparison.

## Side-by-side comparison

| | Dentures | Dental implants |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Fixed or removable | Removable | Fixed in the jaw |
| Chewing strength | About 20–30% of natural teeth | Close to natural teeth |
| Taste and comfort | Plate covers the palate | No plate, natural feel |
| Bone health | Jawbone keeps shrinking | Implants preserve the bone |
| Lifespan | 5–7 years, needs relining | Implants can last a lifetime |
| Upfront cost | Lower | Higher |

## Stability and confidence

Dentures rest on the gums and are held by suction or adhesive. They can slip while eating or talking, and many people avoid hard foods because of it. Implants are anchored in bone, so the teeth on top don't move. Patients often tell us the biggest change is simply not worrying about their teeth any more.

## Bone loss: the hidden cost of dentures

When a tooth is lost, the bone that held it slowly shrinks. Dentures do nothing to stop this, which is why they loosen over time and the face can look sunken. Implants put load back into the bone and help keep it.

## Cost over time

Dentures cost less at the start, but they need relining and replacement every few years, plus adhesives. Implants cost more upfront but can last a lifetime. ## "I was told I don't have enough bone for implants"

This is common among long-term denture wearers. Basal implants anchor in the dense bone deeper in the jaw, so many people who were refused conventional implants can still get fixed teeth, often within 3 days and without bone grafting. Read more about [basal implants](/dental-implants/basal-implants).

## Which should you choose?

Dentures can make sense if your budget is very limited or your health rules out any surgery. For most people who can have implants, fixed teeth give better chewing, comfort and long-term value.
